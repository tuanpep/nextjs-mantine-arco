## Introduction

## Objectives

The original improvement hypothesis [U5](product-design.md#evidence-and-unknowns) is that purpose-based modules and common patterns improve classification/findability without erasing distinct outcomes. It remains untested. The proposed, reversible first-study purpose is comprehension and reported current context, not human acceptance or comparative superiority. C1/C3 investigate comprehension and reported value, but cannot isolate taxonomy effects from clearer labels or completed recording, or establish improvement over alternatives. Comparative commitment remains blocked without equivalent alternative task evidence and actual context. Investigate U1 requester needs, U3 comprehension/access and U2/U4 operational truth. Observe unaided choices, reasoning, correction, moderator assistance, next-action expectations and what people believe was recorded.

Unaided explanation of intent and outcomes can support comprehension of the direction only. Weaken it if confusion persists; consider reversing or narrowing to language/help if actual context suggests existing channels or one entry serve tasks better. These predeclared qualitative criteria inform narrowing or reassessment, not automatic superiority or demand validation. They do not test U5's original comparative improvement hypothesis. No results or interpretations are available. Review findings and limits before commitment; accountable decision owner and follow-up date remain unknown.

C2 covers N1–N4 and UC-04–08 through Support intake/review/confirmation and selected follow-up, with separate observations for each portion. C6 investigates N7–N9/U6/U7 scope clarity and configuration meaning. C1–C4 outcomes cannot establish configuration authority or workspace scope.

C7 investigates N7–N10/U7/U8 reference, settings and work distinctions, including whether catalog maintenance is needed at all. Classification agreement alone cannot establish safety or demand.

C8 investigates N3/N9/N11/U9 task ownership and handoffs across ACT-01–15 and UC-01–26. Conceptual role coverage is not evidence of staffing, permissions or a need for separate accounts.

C9 investigates N3/N12/N13, U10/U11 and UC-05–09/27–29 discussion/action meanings. Audience and saved-limit comprehension does not validate access enforcement or safety.

## Testing strategy

Use participant-based neutral task walkthroughs with the least necessary material: brief purpose descriptions, illustrative records and authored journey text. Use the current demo only for current behavior. Log material/version and moderator help separately from observations. Pilot tasks and accommodations before sessions; the pilot is planned, not completed. Ask what participants expect before explaining intent. Do not label tasks with expected modules. Optional recent incidents require consent and deidentification outside the demo. C5 is an accountable-owner review, not a simulated authority decision.

Prepare a focused first pilot, not one nine-case session. Proposed minimum is C2 intake/follow-up and only the relevant C9 distinction between discussion and an explicit workflow action. Add a short C1 classification exercise when appropriate. Embed C4 preferred-language/accommodation content checks only with reviewed material. Use the case procedures below rather than duplicating full rounds. C6/C7 and broad C8 actor/governance rounds are separate later targeted work; C5 requires actual authority separately. This pilot gathers requester/comprehension evidence only, not permission for module expansion or a substitute for foundation gates C5–C8.

Entry prerequisites are actual likely-user participation and consent, appropriate reviewed language/accommodations, fictional versioned material and a facilitator, currently unknown. Missing prerequisites leave the pilot planned/not run, not ready or booked. Neutral fictional prompts can be: "You cannot sign in to Beacon. What would you need to tell someone to get help?"; "This account has one detail wrong. What would you change before continuing?"; "You return to this request. What happened and what would you do next?" For a relevant C9 extract ask, "What, if anything, changed after this message?" For optional C1 ask, "Where would you begin, and what would you expect to happen?" These are moderator prompts, not invented UI copy or required fields. Ask before teaching labels or expected answers.

Use an unfilled note template with these fields: material/version/date; case/task; unaided response; observed action only if interactive; moderator help; interpretation separated from observations; limitations; next decision. Keep C2 intake observations separate from follow-up observations. Stop for sensitive disclosure, unknown submission outcome or consent withdrawal. Avoid capturing sensitive content and require approved handling; do not infer success/failure or encourage blind retries. Agent dry/read-only checks are not a pilot or participant evidence.

### C2 Understand requester intake and follow-up

Type: evaluation
Status: planned
Owner: unknown
Objective: Investigate N1–N4 and U1 through project/type choice, type-specific detail, review/correction, confirmation/reset meaning and selected next-action comprehension for UC-04–08.
Preconditions: Likely requester participation/consent, appropriate reviewed language/accommodations and facilitator required, all currently unknown. Use fictional Broken login intake material and selected current or illustrative histories with version recorded. Identify current versus proposed material; use the current demo only for current behavior. Source any type-specific details from the recorded material, without inventing required fields or UI copy. Optional deidentified incident account requires consent; availability unknown.
Steps: Intake portion: present a fictional login failure in Beacon, without teaching the expected module/type labels. Ask where they would start, what information they need and what they expect next before explaining intent. Show incomplete type-specific details and ask what they need to add or change. Show a review with a deliberately incorrect fictional detail and ask what they would change before continuing. Follow-up portion, recorded separately: ask what happened, who acts next and what they would do across progress, reply, waiting, Support retest and reopening histories, retaining the superseded outcome. Do not coach or imply notifications. Use only represented outcome/handoff context, not a full handler action exercise.
Expected result: Unaided project/type, required-detail, correction and local-confirmation/reset explanations support intake comprehension; missing-detail confusion or assumed durable/live submission weakens it. Unaided action/outcome boundaries support follow-up framing. Treating retest as resolution or assuming automatic notifications weakens it; unrelated actual pain redirects priority. Interpret the two portions separately. Prose assesses comprehension only, not actual field enforcement, working correction, keyboard behavior, text retention, storage or navigation.
Actual result: not run
Evidence: none, not run
